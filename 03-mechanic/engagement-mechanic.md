# The Mechanic · FinWise

> Module 3 · Retention & Engagement. The engagement mechanic that builds the habit loop, with rationale and a wireframe.

## The mechanic

**Mechanic:** Progress Bar

- **Trigger:** External, to start with: a weekly Monday-morning message when the forecast has gone stale.  "Your forecast is 68% confident. 6 new transactions since last week. Your lowest point may have moved."  FinWise can also send event triggers when something real happens, such as a large payment arriving or an invoice becoming overdue.  Internal, the goal: the owner's own question, "Am I OK for cash?", sends them to FinWise without a prompt. That shift is when the habit has formed. External triggers should fade as it happens: fewer nudges for users who already return on their own.
- **Action:** The smallest step that improves the forecast: review the new transactions in one swipe-through list, with FinWise's guesses pre-filled. Most weeks this is under a minute: confirm, confirm, fix one.  The action has to be easier than the user's current alternative, whether that's checking the bank app and doing the sums in their head or opening a spreadsheet.
- **Reward:** The updated forecast, shown as a change:  "Lowest point moved from £27,900 to £30,200. Harlow Estates paid early."  The reward is variable. Users don't know before they open it whether the news is good, bad or unchanged, and that uncertainty pulls them back. The confidence meter rising to 85% is a secondary reward. The real reward is knowing where they stand.  On bad weeks, the reward is clarity plus a next step: "Cash is tighter in May. Try a what-if." Never a celebration.
- **Investment:** Something that makes next week's forecast better and leaving harder:  Confirm a recurring bill, so it's never asked about again Add an unpaid invoice with its due date Save a what-if, such as "Hire in January" Invite their accountant to review  Each investment loads the next trigger. An invoice due on 20 November triggers "Fernhill was due to pay today. Still expected?" A saved hire scenario triggers "January is 6 weeks away. Still planning to hire?" The more a user invests, the more relevant the triggers become, and the more history they'd lose by leaving.  How it fits together Stage	First session (reaching Aha)	Every week after (keeping Aha) Trigger	The goal picked in onboarding	Stale forecast or a real cash event Action	Connect bank	Review new transactions Reward	First forecast: "7 months of cash"	What changed, and why Investment	Run a what-if, confirm bills	Add invoices, save scenarios, invite accountant What breaks the loop Triggers with nothing behind them. If a user opens a nudge and nothing has changed, the next nudge gets ignored. Only send when the forecast has actually moved or the data is stale. An action that feels like bookkeeping. If reviewing transactions takes 10 minutes, the loop dies. Auto-categorisation accuracy is what keeps the action small. Making the meter the reward. If users chase the score instead of the answer, the habit is built on the wrong thing.  Metric to track: % of users who return within 7 days without a nudge. That's the clearest sign the internal trigger has formed.

_____

## Rationale

A confidence meter is the progress-bar pattern, each step makes the product more useful, not just more used. Moving the meter and getting more value are the same action.
